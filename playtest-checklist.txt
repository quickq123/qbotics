QBOTICS PLAY-TEST CHECKLIST
Work down the list; each step says what should happen. Note anything that doesn't, looks
wrong or feels awkward, and the step number.

START
  1. From g:\MinecraftMods run:   gradlew :neoforge:runPlaytest
     The game opens straight into the "Qbotics Playtest" world (creative, flat), with the
     test stage built beside you. JEI, FTB Chunks and Functional Storage are loaded too.
     (A fresh world: the old one was cleared because plates are now saved differently.)
  2. Find the two computers. The far one with every device beside it is "main"; the near
     one is "remote". Right-click main.

THE COMPUTER
  3. Type:  cat README.txt          It lists every device on the stage and every demo.
  4. Type:  devices                 All of main's devices, with addresses.
  5. Type:  settings                The settings page opens in the editor (top left).
  6. Try copy and paste in the terminal (select text, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V).

THE DEMOS (type each, e.g.  run demos/1_items.py )
  7. 1_items.py      Moves raw iron and coal into the furnace. Run it again later: ingots
                     come out into the store.
  8. 2_filters.py    Counts by tag, wildcard and mod; moves logs to the store.
  9. 3_fluid.py      Water and lava move between the tanks.
 10. 4_energy.py     Energy moves between the battery and the machine.
 11. 5_radio.py      Lists remote's devices and gets a reply from remote's echo program.
 12. 6_devices.py    A table of every device.
 13. 7_sources.py    Pools of sources, keeping gold back, exact amounts.
 14. 8_crafting.py   The crafting table makes planks, then sticks with a Recipe.
 15. 9_world.py      NEW. The lamp blinks three times; cobblestone appears in the space
                     beside the placer, then the breaker breaks it into the store.
 16. 10_levels.py    NEW. The anvil repairs two worn swords and the enchanting table
                     enchants a book; the xp block pays (watch its levels drop).

THE NEW PLATES BY HAND
 17. Find the Logic Pin, Placer and Breaker in the Qbotics creative tab. Each has its own
     look: a slim pin with a red contact, a deep placer, a heavy breaker with teeth.
 18. Put a Logic Pin on a cable next to a redstone lamp, and some redstone dust next to the
     pin's face. The dust should join to the pin, and to no other part of the cable.
 19. In main's terminal, type  python  then:
         from logistics import network
         net = network()
         pin = net.pin("lamp")
         pin.write(15)            the stage's lamp lights
         pin.write(0)             it goes out
         pin.mode = "input"
         pin.read()               0; attach a lever to the lamp, switch it on, read again: 15
         pin.wait_for("block")    then change the lamp (break it, replace it): returns True
 20. Point a Placer at grass or flowing water: it should place there. Point one at a solid
     block: place() should refuse and say why.
 21. Point a Breaker at obsidian (with a store as the destination): it takes about eight
     seconds. At bedrock: it refuses.
 22. Fill the store so it has no room, then break something: nothing breaks, and the error
     names the item that had no room.
 23. Break a chest holding a few items with a Breaker: the chest and everything in it arrive
     in the store (it has nine slots, so keep it to a few kinds).
 24. Pour water next to a cable: the water flows around the cable, not through it.
 25. Sneak and right-click a plate to take it off; break while looking at one plate on a
     busy cable: only that plate comes off.

THE PROBE
 26. Right-click any device with the Network Probe: a pop-up with copyable lines.
 27. Sneak and scroll to switch to Slots mode, then open a chest, a furnace and a crafting
     table: each slot shows its number.
 28. In Slots mode, open a stonecutter with stone in it and click a choice: "Button n" shows
     above the screen, and the same line appears in chat.

OTHER MODS
 29. JEI: hover an item; the probe tooltip still shows the name programs use (Ctrl+C copies).
 30. Functional Storage: put a drawer next to a cable with a Bus on it; try
     net.bus("...").contents() and move() into and out of it.
 31. FTB Chunks: claim a chunk, then try the Placer and Breaker inside and outside the claim.
     (In this test game everything is owned by the same player, "Dev", so the claim will
     allow it; a second player is needed to test a refusal.)

THE FLUID AND ENERGY STORES
 33. Right-click the stage's "tanks" (Fluid Interface): four tanks with sockets above and
     bucket slots below. Hover a tank: it says what it holds and what it takes.
 34. Hold a lava bucket and click tank 2's socket: it shows a lava bucket and now takes only
     lava; your bucket stays in your hand. Click it with an empty hand to clear it.
 35. Put a water bucket in a tank's slot: it empties into that tank and an empty bucket is
     left. Put an empty bucket in: it fills.
 36. Right-click "battery" (Energy Interface): a gauge with the stored FE, and Charge and
     Drain slots. If a mod in the pack has a battery item, put one in each to try them.

WATCHING
 37. In main:  run demos/11_watch.py  then open the stage's chest and move things in and out
     for 30 seconds: each change is printed as it happens (slot, how many, what).

 38. Run  /qbotics stage  again to get the newest demos, then  run demos/12_events.py :
     the lamp blinks once a second, chest changes are printed, and a message sent from
     remote (python, then  import network  and  network.send("<main's address>", "hi") )
     appears at once. It stops by itself after 30 seconds.

LIMITS AND STARTING UP
 39. Type  /qbotics limits : your active computers and antennas, and where each is.
 40. In main's terminal type  shutdown : it switches off. Open it again: it starts afresh.
 41. Leave main on (open it once), quit the game and start the play test again: main and
     remote come back on by themselves (remote's echo is running again).
 42. Optional, to see a computer placed inactive: quit, then open
     neoforge\runs\playtest\config\qbotics-server.toml and set
     computers_per_player = 1 and operators_exempt = false (with cheats on you count as an
     operator, who has no limit). Start again and place a computer: it is placed with a dark
     screen, and right-clicking it says why. Set them back afterwards.

HARD DRIVES
 43. Run  /qbotics stage  again, then in main:  run demos/13_drives.py : a library is written
     to the hard drive "lib" in the bay "shared" and imported from there.
 44. In main's terminal:  ls /net/shared   then  cp /README.txt /net/shared/drive2/  and
     cat /net/shared/drive2/README.txt . The editor's file tree shows /net too.
 45. Right-click the Drive Bay beside main: take a drive out (its folder goes from /net within
     half a second), put it back (its files are still there).
 46. Craft a Hard Drive, name it in an anvil, put it in the bay: it appears under that name.

 47. In main:  cat requirements.txt   (it points at remote's repository), then
     pip available --source <remote's IP>/repo/lib   and   pip install -r requirements.txt :
     stock and greetings are installed. Then  python ,  import stock ,  print(stock.report()) .
 48. pip list ,  pip uninstall greetings ,  pip list  again.
 49. On remote, retune its antenna (python, then  import network ,
     network.Radio().tune("other") ) and in main try  pip install -r requirements.txt  again:
     it says why it can't reach the repository. Tune it back with  .tune("1") .

REMOTE
 50. In main:  ssh  followed by remote's address from README.txt. You are now typing on
     remote; Ctrl+C stops its echo; exit comes back.

PROGRAMMER (build this one yourself, from the creative tab)
 51. Place a Programmer by clicking the side of a block: its contact-pad face points at that
     block. Right-click it, put a Hard Drive in, press Edit files: the editor opens with no
     terminal and no Run button.
 52. On the drive make folders basic and miner, each with a startup.py that prints its name,
     and a programs.txt with the lines   3: miner   and   *: basic .
 53. Place a computer in front of the pad: it starts and prints basic (the * program), and
     the hotbar message says what was installed.
 54. Put a lever or redstone at level 3 into the Programmer (e.g. a comparator, or a lever and
     12 blocks of dust): when it turns on, the computer restarts printing miner. Leaving it on
     does nothing more.
 55. Place a second Programmer facing the same computer, and power both from one lever: only
     one programs it (the other says "already programmed this tick").
 56. On a computer cabled to a Programmer:  python , then
     from logistics import network ;  p = network().programmer() ;  print(p.status) ;
     p.program("basic") .

DISPLAYS (build these yourself, from the creative tab)
 57. Place six Text Monitors in a 3 wide, 2 high wall (place each against the face side of the
     last so they all face the same way). They join into one screen. Cable a computer to the
     back of it. Probe any block of it: it is one "monitor"; name it "wall".
 58. In the computer:  python , then  from logistics import network ;  mon = network().monitor() ;
     mon.size()  (45, 20) ;  mon.print("hello")  a few times ;
     mon.write_at(0, 5, "status: ok", "lime", "blue") ;  mon.scale = 2 ;  mon.scale = 0.5 .
 59. Break one corner block, then put it back: the screen keeps its name "wall" and the text
     in its top rows (rows the screen lost while smaller are blank again).
 60. Place four Control Panels in a 2x2 square beside the computer (cabled). In python:
     p = network().panel() ;  t = p.toggle("Pump", at=(0, 0), span=(3, 1)) ;
     g = p.gauge("Power", 0, 100, at=(0, 1), span=(8, 1)) ;  g.value = 70 ;
     s = p.slider("Speed", 0, 10, at=(0, 2), span=(8, 1)) ;  b = p.button("Go", at=(4, 0)) .
 61. Right-click the toggle and the slider on the block: they change. Sneak + right-click with
     an empty hand: the close-up view. Drag the slider there. Back in python:  t.on ,  s.value .
 62. A program that answers:  import events , then
     b.on_press(lambda e: print("pressed by", e.player)) ,  events.after(30, events.stop) ,
     events.run()  and press Go a few times within 30 seconds.
 63. Look at the screens at night (/time set midnight): they light themselves.

ANTENNA UPGRADES (build these yourself)
 64. Right-click an antenna: its screen has two upgrade slots and a "Chunk loading" button.
     Put a Transport Upgrade and a Chunk Loader Upgrade in it (creative tab).
 65. Build a second network 15-20 blocks away (a computer is not needed there): an antenna
     with a Transport Upgrade, cabled to an Interface with a coal filter and some coal.
     Note its external address (right-click it).
 66. At your computer:  python ,  from logistics import network, move ,  net = network() ,
     far = net.remote("<its address>") ,  far.devices() ,
     move(far.interface(), net.interface(), "coal", 16) . (Your interface needs a coal filter.)
 67. Take the Transport Upgrade out of the far antenna and try the move again: refused, saying
     which antenna needs one.
 68. Chunk loading: the antenna's screen says how many chunks it holds. Fly 300+ blocks away
     and come back: furnaces on that network kept smelting. Press the button to switch it off;
     in python:  import network ,  network.Radio().chunks() .

QBOTS (from the creative tab)
 69. Place an Iron Qbot: it faces you, hovers and bobs. Right-click it: its 4x4 inventory,
     hand and upgrade slots, energy gauge, two tanks, and Run / Pause / Stop. Run says
     "No program: install one with a Programmer".
 70. A Qbot has no terminal: its program comes from a Programmer. Put a Programmer facing it,
     Edit files on the Programmer's drive, write startup.py:  import qbot ,  qbot.forward() ,
     qbot.turn_right() ,  print(qbot.position()) . Power the Programmer: the Qbot starts. Watch it
     glide and turn; its screen shows what it printed. Steps 71-75 are programs the same way.
 70b. A long program (a loop with time.sleep): Pause holds it, Run says Resume and carries on;
     Stop ends it. Break a line in the program (a typo): the screen says it stopped with an error.
 70c. Log (corner of the program strip): everything it printed, read-only. Scroll back; Escape
     or Back returns. The error's whole traceback is there. Drag across some lines, Ctrl+C,
     and paste them somewhere; with nothing selected, Copy all copies everything.
 71. Put stone in front:  qbot.dig()  (its arms swing; cobblestone goes into its slots).
     Obsidian in front:  qbot.dig()  says it needs a better tool. Put a diamond pickaxe in its
     hand (screen or  from qbot import tools ,  tools.equip("diamond_pickaxe") ) and dig again.
 72. Water and lava: put a source of each beside it:  qbot.pump("left") ,  qbot.pump("right") ;
     its screen shows them in separate tanks. qbot.pour("front", "water") .
 73. A chest in front:  from qbot import inventory ,  inventory.drop()  then  inventory.read() .
     A furnace beside it:  inventory.drop("front", "coal", 4, face="left")  fills the fuel.
 74. qbot.use() on a lever; with shears in its hand, qbot.use() on a sheep.
 75. A Chunk Loader Upgrade in its upgrade slot: walk far away while it digs a tunnel.
 76. Break it: its items fall out; place it again and its files, energy and fluid are kept.

SERIAL BUSES (Input and Output are gone)

 78. Craft a Universal Serial Bus (USB) from one of each; it is royal purple on the cable.
     Two USBs:  move(net.usb("a"), net.usb("b"), "lava", 1000)  moves fluid between them.

     Mekanism or Thermal machine):  python ,  from logistics import network, move ,

 80. move(net.interface(), m, "raw_iron", 8)  sends items;  move(net.energy_interface(), m,
     count=10000)  sends energy, through the same plate.
 81. Old worlds: Input and Output plates already on cables are now Serial Buses and keep
     working; Input and Output items in chests are gone.

SSH GATEWAY (VS Code)
 95. In the world's serverconfig/qbotics-server.toml set [ssh_gateway] enabled = true; restart the
     world. /qbotics ssh says it is on.
 96. On the PC: ssh-keygen -t ed25519 (if you have no key). In game: /qbotics ssh key add <the line
     in id_ed25519.pub>.
 97. PowerShell: ssh <a computer's name>@localhost -p 2222 : its terminal. Type ls, run a program,
     Ctrl+C it. ssh laptop@localhost -p 2222 reaches your laptop.
 98. VS Code, SSH FS extension: add the computer as a host (see reference 7f), open its files, edit
     and save one, open its terminal. Then the SFTP extension on a local folder, and git init there.
